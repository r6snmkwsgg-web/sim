Why bar closes lie

The drawdown limit is a property of the price path, not of the closes. Here is one long trade over three bars. Read only the closes and it is a pass. Read the path and the account was liquidated on bar 1 and never saw bar 2.

  • bar 1 · low96.00  →  −$400
  • bar 1 · close101.00  →  +$100
  • bar 2 · close120.00  →  +$2,000

The rulebook is worth more than the strategy

The same three strategies, the same market, the same costs — read the $300 limit as trailing behind your equity high-water mark, or as a fixed floor at $19,700. That single reading moves the pass rate by an order of magnitude.

Intervals are a day-cluster bootstrap, not a plain binomial one: overlapping 24-hour windows drawn from the same few months are nowhere near independent samples, and a binomial interval computed as if they were is several times too narrow — in the flattering direction.

05

What the simulation says

Under a trailing floor, nothing survives

Every zero-edge strategy lands near half a percent, and around 99% of failures are drawdown breaches rather than timeouts. The 5:1 ratio between target and limit is not the binding constraint — the ratchet is. Get $500 ahead and you have $300 of room from there, not $800.

Under a static floor it is priced near fair

Buy-and-hold lands around 10%, straddling breakeven. That is not a strategy: it is the gambler's ruin bound, 300/1800 = 16.7%, less what the 24-hour clock and the costs take out. The fee looks set close to the fair value of pure variance.

Trading more makes it worse

Fixed TP/SL takes the most entries and does worst under both readings — it pays round-turn friction repeatedly into a $300 allowance. At 45% size roughly 23 round turns is the whole account, before the market has moved at all.

This is a synthetic market

The price series is generated on load: driftless — a martingale, so no edge is baked in — with volatility clustering, jumps, session-shaped intraday volatility and a spread that widens when liquidity thins. Bars are aggregated from simulated sub-bar steps, never decorated onto a close, because the intrabar extremes are the answer here.

It characterises the machinery and the rulebook. It is not a claim about EURUSD. The repository ships a Dukascopy tick fetcher — real bid/ask, back to 2003 — and the same engine reads it unchanged.

06

Point it at real prices

The Python engine this is ported from carries 99 tests, needs no third-party packages to run them, and is validated end to end against gambler's ruin. 

python run_tests.py                    # 99 tests, no installs, ~4s
python -m propsim fetch --symbol EURUSD --start 2023-01-01 --end 2025-01-01
python -m propsim mc    --symbol EURUSD --attempts 10000 --workers 8
python -m propsim sweep --symbol EURUSD --out out/EURUSD.png

Two years gives roughly 500 independent 24-hour windows instead of the few dozen a month of data provides — which is what the interval above is really reporting, and what decides whether any of this means anything.
